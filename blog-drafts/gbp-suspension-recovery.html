The 3 Most Common Reasons Hampton Roads Contractors Get Suspended

1. Address Violations (The #1 Cause in the 757)

This is by far the most common reason home service contractors in Virginia Beach, Norfolk, and Chesapeake get suspended. Google requires that service-area businesses either show a physical address where customers can visit you, OR hide your address entirely and only show your service area.

The mistake most contractors make is displaying a residential address or a virtual office address. If Google suspects you are using a P.O. box, a UPS store mailbox, a residential home, or a shared coworking space as your business address, your profile gets flagged.

The fix: If you work from home or do not have a storefront that customers visit, Google's guidelines are clear — hide your address in GBP settings and set your service area to the cities you serve. This is not a loophole. It is the actual rule.

2. Category Misdirection

Google looks at your GBP category to understand what you do. If a Norfolk electrician selects "Electrical Contractor" but their description talks extensively about HVAC, Google's algorithm gets confused and flags the listing as misleading.

The fix: Choose the most specific primary category for your exact services. For plumbers in Chesapeake, "Plumber" is better than "Home Services." For roofers in Suffolk, "Roofing Contractor" is better than "General Contractor." Your primary category should match 80% of your business.

3. Suspicious Review Activity

This one catches a lot of Hampton Roads contractors. If you get 8 five-star reviews in a week after having zero for six months, Google notices. If the reviews are from accounts with no profile photos or review history, Google flags them. If multiple reviews come from the same IP address, Google suspends the listing.

The fix: Generate reviews naturally and consistently. Aim for 3-5 reviews per month, not 20 in a week. Ask every satisfied customer to leave a review, but do not incentivize or batch them.

Step-by-Step GBP Recovery Process

1

Diagnose the Violation

Before you appeal, you need to know what you are appealing. Log into your GBP dashboard and look for the notification explaining the suspension. Common messages include "Suspicious activity detected," "Business location does not meet guidelines," or "Deceptive content or practices." If the notification is vague (it usually is), check your address settings, category selection, and recent review activity first.

2

Fix the Issue

If your address was visible and you work from home, go to GBP settings → Info → set your address to hidden → select your service area. If your category was too broad, change it to the most specific option. If reviews were batched, slow down your review generation for two weeks before appealing.

3

Prepare Your Appeal Documentation

Google will ask for verification documents. Have these ready:

  • Business license or professional license (state of Virginia)
  • Utility bill showing your business address (within 90 days)
  • Tax registration certificate
  • Photos of your vehicle with visible business branding — this matters more than most people realize
  • Photos of you working at a customer's location
4

Submit the Appeal

Go to the GBP dashboard and click "Appeal." Fill out the form completely. Do not write one sentence. Explain: what your business does, what caused the violation (even if you disagree with it), what you changed to fix it, and that you understand and will follow Google's guidelines going forward. Attach your documentation and submit.

5

Wait and Monitor

Google typically responds within 3-7 business days. Some appeals process faster, some take up to two weeks. Check your GBP dashboard daily. Do not submit multiple appeals — that actually slows the process down.

How to Never Get Suspended Again

Do These Every Month:

  • Check your address settings — make sure they match Google's current guidelines
  • Monitor your review velocity — 3-6 reviews per month is the sweet spot
  • Keep your business info consistent — GBP name, address, and phone must match your website and citation listings

Don't Do These:

  • Do not use a virtual office, UPS mailbox, or residential address as your storefront address
  • Do not change your business name, category, or phone number more than once per quarter
  • Do not ask friends or family to leave reviews — Google can detect network connections
  • Do not post the same photo across multiple GBP categories

What to Do While You Wait for Reinstatement

Your phone will be quieter while your listing is down. Here is what Hampton Roads contractors should do during a suspension:

  1. Boost Google Ads temporarily — run a campaign targeting your service + city keywords to fill the gap
  2. Post on Nextdoor — Hampton Roads homeowners trust Nextdoor recommendations. A genuine post can generate leads while your GBP is down
  3. Call past customers — a simple "just checking in" call can generate immediate work
  4. Review your website — make sure it is optimized to convert the traffic you are paying for with ads
